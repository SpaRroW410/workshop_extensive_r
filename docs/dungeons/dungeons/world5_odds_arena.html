World 5 · Odds Arena

Odds, odds ratios, model comparison, ROC and calibration, OR vs PR

Briefing

g <- glm(htn ~ salt_g + age + bmi + sex, data = cc, family = binomial)
tidy(g, exponentiate = TRUE, conf.int = TRUE) |>
  mutate(across(where(is.numeric), \(x) round(x, 2)))
# A tibble: 5 × 7
  term        estimate std.error statistic p.value conf.low conf.high
  <chr>          <dbl>     <dbl>     <dbl>   <dbl>    <dbl>     <dbl>
1 (Intercept)     0         1.06     -9.78       0     0         0   
2 salt_g          1.25      0.06      4.01       0     1.12      1.4 
3 age             1.1       0.01      7.72       0     1.07      1.12
4 bmi             1.12      0.03      3.41       0     1.05      1.2 
5 sexMale         2.34      0.22      3.82       0     1.52      3.64

Odds and odds ratios. Odds = p / (1 − p). A logistic coefficient is a log-odds difference; exponentiate it to get an odds ratio and its CI. An OR compares odds, not probabilities.

c(AIC = AIC(g), AUC = as.numeric(pROC::auc(pROC::roc(g$y, fitted(g), quiet = TRUE))))
        AIC         AUC 
534.5469254   0.8133937

Judging a model. Lower AIC is better (same data, penalty built in). AUC measures discrimination: the chance a case is ranked above a non-case. Calibration asks whether predicted risks match observed ones. A pseudo-R² is not variance explained.

OR vs PR. When the outcome is common (roughly above 10%), the OR is further from 1 than the prevalence ratio. Do not read it as “times as likely”; report a PR (log-binomial or Poisson with robust SE) or convert from the baseline risk.

Level 1 · Odds and odds ratios

In a village 20% of adults are hypertensive. What are the odds of hypertension? (2 decimals)

A logistic model gives a log-odds coefficient of 0.405 for tobacco use. What is the odds ratio? (1 decimal)

#> term       OR   conf.low conf.high
#> tobaccoYes 1.50   1.10     2.05

Which reading of the tobacco row is correct (hypertension outcome, other variables fixed)?

  • Tobacco raises the chance of hypertension by 1.5 percentage points
  • Users have 1.5 times the odds of hypertension (CI 1.10–2.05)
  • Users are 1.5 times as likely to be hypertensive as non-users
  • Users have a 50% probability of hypertension

Level 2 · Comparing and judging models

Model A (age, salt) has AIC = 410 and Model B (age, salt, BMI) has AIC = 402, both fitted to the same 580 people. What is the sensible conclusion?

  • Model B fits better even after the penalty for extra terms
  • Model B is better only because it has more parameters
  • Model A is better because it has fewer predictors to estimate
  • The AICs cannot be compared without a p-value

A logistic model has McFadden R² = 0.15 and an AUC of 0.78. Which statement is correct?

  • It explains 15% of the variance in hypertension
  • It is poorly calibrated because R² is below 0.2
  • It ranks cases above non-cases about 78% of the time
  • It classifies 78% of the people into the right category

A risk model has AUC = 0.85, yet among people it assigns 40% risk only 20% develop the disease. This is a problem of…

  • calibration, despite good discrimination
  • non-linearity in the outcome
  • discrimination, despite good calibration
  • collinearity among the predictors

Level 3 · OR to PR and workflow

In unexposed adults 20% have hypertension and the adjusted OR for exposure is 2.0. Using the odds of the unexposed, what is the prevalence ratio? (1 decimal)

Put a sensible logistic-regression analysis in order, from first to last:

  1. Decide whether OR or PR is the right measure to report
  2. Confirm the outcome is binary and check events per variable
  3. Check discrimination with AUC and calibration
  4. Report ORs or PRs with CIs, not only p-values
  5. Fit the model chosen from the causal diagram
  6. Exponentiate coefficients and read ORs with their CIs

Boss · Convert an OR story into a PR story

Boss: flag every flawed sentence in this write-up (hypertension is common: 30% prevalence; adjusted OR for tobacco = 2.0, 95% CI 1.4–2.9).

  • Tobacco users had twice the odds of hypertension (adjusted OR 2.0, 95% CI 1.4–2.9).
  • So tobacco users were twice as likely to be hypertensive.
  • With a prevalence of 30% the OR overstates the prevalence ratio, so a PR should also be reported.
  • The model explains 20% of the variance in hypertension (McFadden R² = 0.20).
  • The model discriminated moderately well (AUC 0.74) and calibration was checked in a plot.
  • The coefficient for tobacco was 2.0 on the log-odds scale.
