Region 2 · Explore by Picture

Distributions, outliers, overplotting, facets and the pattern of missing data

Briefing

Read first: Best Practices and Matching Data to Plots. Plot before you model: pictures find skew, outliers, errors and missingness that a table of means hides.

ggplot(ncd, aes(sbp)) + geom_histogram(bins = 30, fill = "grey70", colour = "white") + facet_wrap(~ sex) + labs(x = "Systolic BP (mmHg)")
ggplot(ncd, aes(age, sbp)) + geom_point(alpha = 0.35) + geom_smooth(colour = "red") + labs(x = "Age (years)", y = "SBP (mmHg)")
ncd |> group_by(residence) |> summarise(pct_bmi_missing = round(100 * mean(is.na(bmi)), 1))
# A tibble: 2 × 2
  residence pct_bmi_missing
  <fct>               <dbl>
1 Urban                   5
2 Rural                   2

Reading the pictures. A long right tail: mean above median, so report median and IQR. Dots beyond the box-plot whiskers are unusual, not wrong: check them. Dense scatter plots need transparency (alpha) and a smooth trend. Facets put groups on the same axes. Tabulate missingness by group before a complete-case analysis.

Level 1 · Distributions

A histogram of triglycerides has a long tail to the right and the mean is larger than the median. What is the best way to summarise and analyse it?

  • Delete the highest values until the histogram looks quite symmetrical
  • Convert the variable to a factor so that the shape no longer matters
  • Report the mean and SD, since the mean always describes the centre
  • Report the median and IQR; consider a log scale or a rank test

A box plot of SBP shows five points above the upper whisker. What should you do?

  • Check each record: it could be an error or a real extreme value
  • Delete the five points, because points above a whisker are errors
  • Replace them with the median so that the plot looks much cleaner
  • Ignore them, because box plots only ever show the middle half of data

SBP has Q1 = 118 and Q3 = 142 mmHg. A box plot draws points beyond Q3 + 1.5 × IQR as separate dots. Above what SBP value is a point drawn as a dot? (whole number)

Level 2 · Choosing the picture

A scatter plot of age against SBP for 600 adults is a dense blob in which many points overlap. What helps most?

  • Plot only the first 50 rows of the data instead of all 600 adults
  • Make points partly transparent (alpha) and add a smooth trend line
  • Remove the axis labels so that the blob looks a little less crowded
  • Increase the size of the points so that each one is easier to see

You want the distribution of SBP separately for men and women, side by side, with the same axes. Which is the clearest choice?

  • One histogram of SBP with the sexes pooled into a single group
  • Two histograms in panels (facet_wrap) or box plots by sex
  • A line chart of SBP drawn in the order of the participant ID
  • A pie chart showing the number of men and the number of women

BMI is missing for 12% of rural adults but only 2% of urban adults. What does this suggest before a complete-case analysis?

  • The missing values are random and can be ignored without any check
  • BMI should be dropped from the whole study and replaced by weight
  • Rural adults have a BMI of zero, so a zero should be entered
  • Missingness depends on residence, so complete cases may be biased

Boss · Review an exploratory report

Boss: flag every flawed statement in this exploratory-analysis report.

  • The SBP histogram is slightly right-skewed, so the median and IQR were reported.
  • Points above the box-plot whiskers were deleted because they are outliers.
  • BMI is missing for 4% of participants, and missingness was tabulated by sex and residence.
  • A scatter plot with transparent points showed a gentle upward trend of SBP with age.
  • Because the plots show a link between salt and SBP, salt intake causes high blood pressure.
  • Facets were used so that men and women could be compared on the same axes.
