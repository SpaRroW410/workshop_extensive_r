  • must be re-estimated as a percentage change

  • sex is a mediator, so adjusting for it is a mistake
  • the sex-adjusted model contains a calculation error somewhere
  • sex confounds the association and explains much of the slope
  • alcohol has no real effect once sex is included in the model

With no schooling as the reference, primary = −1.0 and graduate = −3.0 mmHg. How many mmHg lower is graduate than primary? (1 decimal)

Which practice best protects against bias when choosing covariates?

  • Keep whichever variables are significant on their own
  • Pre-specify them from a causal diagram before seeing results
  • Add all available variables to maximise R-squared
  • Let stepwise selection decide and report the final model alone

  • Non-independence of the observations collected
  • Inflated standard errors and unstable coefficients
  • Residuals that clearly fail the normality assumption
  • A curved relationship with the outcome variable

The adjusted OR for high versus low activity is 0.55 (95% CI 0.33–0.95). Which wording is correct?

  • The CI lower limit is below 1, so it is not significant
  • Activity prevents 45% of cases in this population
  • High-activity adults were 45% less likely overall
  • High-activity adults had 45% lower odds, adjusted

Flag every flawed sentence from this results paragraph (cross-sectional study, 40% prevalence).

  • Rural residents had 1.8 times the odds of anaemia (95% CI 1.2–2.7).
  • So rural residents were 1.8 times as likely to be anaemic.
  • Each additional year of schooling was associated with 0.12 g/dL higher Hb (95% CI 0.05–0.19).
  • The model explains 31% of the variance in anaemia (McFadden R² = 0.31).
  • Wealth was kept because stepwise selection retained it.
  • Age had β = 0.04.

A registry of 90,000 women finds that each extra 10 g/day of fibre goes with 0.4 kg lower weight (95% CI 0.3–0.5, p < 0.001). What is the best reading?

  • The finding is unreliable because the registry is so large
  • The tiny p-value proves fibre is a powerful weight-loss tool
  • The CI excludes zero, so women will notice a clear change in weight
  • The estimate is precise, but the difference is small in practice

  • include fasting glucose to improve the precision of the estimate
  • include fasting glucose and then drop obesity itself
  • include fasting glucose because adjusting always reduces bias
  • leave out fasting glucose, which lies on the causal path
