World 6 · The Reporting Guild

Results wording, common pitfalls, overfitting, reviewer replies

Briefing

m_lin <- lm(sbp ~ salt_g + age + bmi + sex, data = cc)
tbl_regression(m_lin, intercept = FALSE) |> bold_labels()
Characteristic Beta 95% CI p-value
salt_g 1.8 1.3, 2.4 <0.001
age 0.59 0.48, 0.71 <0.001
bmi 0.86 0.50, 1.2 <0.001
sex


    Female — —
    Male 4.6 2.3, 6.9 <0.001
Abbreviation: CI = Confidence Interval

A results sentence has five parts: direction, size in units, comparison or reference, a 95% CI, and what was adjusted for. “Significant predictor, p < 0.001” is not a result.

The pitfalls. Stepwise selection or “p < 0.2” screens as the analysis; β without units or reference; an OR read as “times as likely” for a common outcome; a pseudo-R² called variance explained; statistical significance called clinical importance; overfitting (aim for about 10 or more events per variable); ignored clustering; causal words from cross-sectional data.

Answering a reviewer. Change the analysis or its framing, do not merely defend it: pre-specify covariates, report one model, label exploratory work as exploratory, validate prediction models, and state limitations.

Level 1 · Say it properly

Which results sentence reports a linear-regression slope properly (SBP in mmHg, salt in g/day, adjusted model)?

  • 1.8 mmHg higher mean SBP per extra g/day of salt (95% CI 1.2–2.4), adjusted
  • Salt was a significant predictor of SBP in the adjusted model (β = 1.8, p < 0.001)
  • SBP rose by 1.8 for each person who ate more salt in the survey (p < 0.001)
  • Salt explained 1.8% of the change in SBP among the adults after adjustment

Sex is coded Female = 0, Male = 1 and its coefficient is 3.8. Which wording is complete and correct?

  • Men had 3.8 mmHg higher mean SBP than women, others fixed
  • Men scored 3.8 mmHg above the overall average SBP of all adults
  • Sex had a β of 3.8 mmHg for the male group in the fitted model
  • Being male raised SBP by 3.8 mmHg in each man in the survey

A logistic model has 8 predictor parameters and 80 people with the outcome (the smaller group). What is the number of events per variable? (whole number)

Level 2 · Pitfalls

A thesis screens covariates and keeps only those with univariable p < 0.2. Physical activity has a univariable p near 1 but changes the age-adjusted OR for the exposure by 20%. What went wrong?

  • The p-value threshold was applied to the wrong test
  • Physical activity should have been entered as a mediator
  • The screen discarded a confounder that crude tests hid
  • The screen was too lenient and kept noise variables

A risk model built on 300 people with 60 candidate predictors has AUC = 0.90 on those 300 and AUC = 0.55 in a new sample. This pattern shows…

  • that the new sample has too few events to judge anything
  • collinearity among the 60 predictors in both samples
  • good calibration but poor discrimination in the new sample
  • overfitting: the model learned noise in its own data

Level 3 · Reviewers and wording

Reviewer #2 writes: ‘Covariates were chosen by backward stepwise selection. Please justify.’ Which reply is strongest?

  • We now report only the variables that stayed significant in the final stepwise model
  • We repeated the stepwise search using BIC instead of AIC and the same variables were kept
  • Stepwise selection is widely used in published work, so we made no change to the analysis
  • Covariates now come from a causal diagram; stepwise output is labelled exploratory

From a single cross-sectional survey, which sentence is acceptable?

  • Quitting tobacco will lower the SBP of adults by 3 mmHg
  • Tobacco users had a mean SBP 3 mmHg higher than non-users
  • Tobacco use increases systolic BP by about 3 mmHg in adults
  • Tobacco is a cause of raised SBP in this whole population

Put a pre-submission reporting routine in order, from first to last:

  1. State how covariates and the model were chosen
  2. Add checks: assumptions, clustering, overfitting, fit
  3. Word associations carefully and note limitations
  4. Name the software and package versions
  5. Describe the design, sample and missing data
  6. Report estimates with units, CIs and reference groups

Boss · Reviewer #2 simulator

Boss: you are Reviewer #2. Flag every flawed sentence in this results paragraph (cross-sectional survey, 600 adults in 20 villages; hypertension 32%).

  • Adults aged 50 or more had 5.1 times the odds of hypertension (95% CI 3.4–7.6), adjusted for sex, BMI and residence.
  • This means older adults were 5.1 times as likely to be hypertensive.
  • The model explains 29% of the variance in hypertension (Tjur R² = 0.29).
  • Salt intake causes hypertension, as shown by the adjusted OR of 1.3 per g/day.
  • Standard errors were adjusted for the 20 villages using cluster-robust methods.
  • Rural residents differed by 3.5 mmHg in mean SBP, which was significant (p = 0.01) and therefore important.
