Pre-test · Baseline check

20 questions across the core topics, Worlds 1–6. No feedback while you answer.

This baseline is not graded. Answer from what you know; rate your confidence honestly. Your skill breakdown shows which worlds to prioritise, and your post-test later shows your gain. Order of options is shuffled every time.

The number of dengue cases reported per ward per month is the outcome. Which family is the natural starting point?

  • Poisson or negative binomial regression
  • Cox proportional hazards regression for survival
  • Multinomial logistic regression
  • Ordinal logistic regression for ordered categories

In a model for systolic BP, BMI has β = 0.9. Which statement is correct?

  • Mean SBP is 0.9 mmHg higher per extra BMI unit, others fixed
  • Each person’s SBP rises 0.9 mmHg if their BMI rises 1
  • SBP is 0.9 percent higher for each extra BMI unit in the sample
  • BMI explains 0.9 of the variance in blood pressure

The residual SD is 12 mmHg. About how many mmHg is the half-width of a 95% prediction interval for one new patient? (whole number)

Adding ten pure-noise variables to a regression will usually…

  • lower R² because noise adds extra error
  • leave R² unchanged but widen the confidence intervals
  • raise R² a little but leave adjusted R² flat or lower
  • raise both R² and adjusted R² by a clearly visible margin

BMI is centred at 25 kg/m² before fitting. The intercept now represents…

  • the expected outcome for a person with BMI 25
  • the average outcome across all participants
  • the expected outcome at a BMI of zero
  • the change in the outcome for each extra unit of BMI

The crude β of salt on SBP is 5.0; adjusted for age it is 2.0. The most sensible reading is that…

  • age confounds the association and explains much of the slope
  • the adjusted model contains a calculation error somewhere in it
  • salt has no real effect once age is included in the model
  • age is a mediator, so adjusting for it is a mistake

With urban as the reference, rural = +4.0 and semi-urban = +1.5 mmHg. How many mmHg higher is rural than semi-urban? (1 decimal)

A thesis reports the final model of backward stepwise selection with no other justification. The main objection is that…

  • AIC cannot be computed for linear regression models
  • backward selection can only ever add new variables to the model
  • stepwise selection forbids adjusting for any confounder
  • p-values and CIs ignore the search and look too optimistic

Patients are drawn from 8 hospitals and share unmeasured hospital effects. Which assumption is most at risk?

  • Absence of influential points
  • Linearity of the age effect
  • Independence of observations
  • Normality of the outcome variable

Disease prevalence is 20%. What are the odds of disease? (2 decimals)

The adjusted OR for smoking is 0.60 (95% CI 0.45–0.80). Which wording is correct?

  • Smokers had 40% lower odds of the outcome, adjusted
  • Smokers were 40% less likely to have the outcome
  • The OR is below 1, so the result is not significant
  • Smoking prevents 40% of cases in this population

Flag every flawed sentence from this results paragraph (cross-sectional study, 32% prevalence).

  • Men had 2.1 times the odds of hypertension (95% CI 1.3–3.5).
  • Therefore men were 2.1 times as likely to be hypertensive.
  • Each year of age was associated with 0.6 mmHg higher SBP (95% CI 0.5–0.7).
  • The model explains 29% of the variance in hypertension (Tjur R² = 0.29).
  • Salt was kept because backward stepwise selection retained it.
  • BMI had β = 0.88.

A trial of 40,000 patients finds that a new drug lowers SBP by 0.5 mmHg more than placebo (95% CI 0.3–0.7, p < 0.001). What is the best reading?

  • The CI excludes zero, so patients will notice the improvement
  • The tiny p-value shows the drug has a large clinical benefit
  • The result is unreliable because the sample is so large
  • The effect is precisely estimated but clinically negligible

Rural residence raises SBP partly through higher salt intake. To estimate the TOTAL effect of rural residence on SBP, the model should…

  • leave out salt intake, which lies on the causal path
  • include salt intake and then drop rural residence itself
  • include salt intake to improve the precision of the estimate
  • include salt intake because adjusting always reduces bias

Residuals against fitted values for a model of SBP form a narrow band at low fitted values that widens steadily to the right. What is the best next step?

  • Switch to a model that assumes normal residuals
  • Add a squared term for every predictor in the model
  • Delete the participants with the largest fitted values
  • Use robust standard errors or transform the outcome

One record shows a weight of 720 kg and has a Cook’s distance far above all others. Field notes say weights were sometimes typed in pounds. What is the best action?

  • Delete the record quietly so the slope looks stable
  • Leave it alone, because one record cannot matter in 600
  • Add a quadratic term until the record stops standing out
  • Verify it against the source form, correct it and refit

A logistic model has AUC = 0.62. Which statement is correct?

  • Cases get a higher risk than non-cases in 62% of random pairs
  • 62% of the people in the sample are classified correctly
  • Predicted risks are 62% higher than the observed risks on average
  • The model explains 62% of the variance in the outcome

In unexposed adults 30% have the outcome and the adjusted OR for exposure is 2.0. Using the unexposed odds, what is the prevalence ratio? (1 decimal)

A logistic model has 6 predictor parameters and 54 people with the outcome (the smaller group). How many events per variable is that? (whole number)

A cross-sectional survey finds an adjusted SBP difference of 4.2 mmHg (95% CI 1.1–7.3) for alcohol use. Which sentence reports it best?

  • Drinking explains 4.2% of the variation in SBP among the adults sampled
  • Drinkers had 4.2 mmHg higher mean SBP (95% CI 1.1–7.3), adjusted
  • Alcohol use raised the SBP of drinkers by 4.2 mmHg (95% CI 1.1–7.3)
  • Alcohol was a significant predictor of the SBP in this survey (β = 4.2)
