World 4 · Assumption Ruins

Linearity, independence, normality, equal variance, collinearity, influence

Briefing

m <- lm(sbp ~ salt_g + age + bmi + sex, data = cc)
check_model(m, check = c("linearity", "homogeneity", "qq", "outliers"))

Read the plot, name the assumption. A curve in residuals vs fitted means non-linearity; a funnel means unequal variance; a heavy-tailed Q–Q plot means non-normality; a few extreme Cook’s distances mean influential points. Residuals grouped by village mean non-independence.

Match the fix to the violation. Curve: add a term or spline. Funnel: robust SEs, transformation or weights. Clusters: mixed model, GEE or cluster-robust SEs. Collinearity (VIF around 10): keep one variable, chosen from the question. Influence: check the record, then show results with and without.

Size matters more than tests. In large samples, normality tests flag trivial departures and slope CIs stay approximately valid; normality matters most for prediction intervals and small samples.

Level 1 · Reading the plots

A residuals-vs-fitted plot for sbp ~ age shows a clear U-shaped smoother instead of a flat line near zero. What does this indicate?

  • The straight-line form is wrong for age
  • The outcome has too many missing values
  • The residuals are not normally distributed
  • The observations are not independent

Residuals are tight around zero for young adults and fan out widely for older adults. Which pair is the correct diagnosis and a reasonable response?

  • Non-linearity; use robust standard errors
  • Unequal variance; drop the oldest adults from the data
  • Non-normality; add a squared age term
  • Unequal variance; use robust standard errors

Regressing waist on all the other predictors gives R² = 0.80. What is the VIF for waist? (whole number)

Level 2 · Collinearity and clusters

BMI and waist (r = 0.95) are both in a model and each has a VIF near 10. Which is the best response?

  • Keep both, because the overall model fit is good
  • Standardise both variables and keep both
  • Keep whichever has the smaller p-value
  • Keep one, chosen from the research question

600 adults were sampled from 20 villages, and residents of one village resemble each other. If this is ignored in a plain linear model, the usual…

  • standard errors are too small and CIs too narrow
  • R² is inflated but the SEs stay valid
  • coefficients are biased towards zero in every case
  • residuals become perfectly normal in each village

Level 3 · Judgement calls

With n = 600 the residual Q–Q plot shows mild skew in the upper tail, and the Shapiro–Wilk test gives p = 0.01. For the CIs of the slopes, the sensible conclusion is…

  • Invalid; switch to a non-parametric test of the slope
  • Invalid; transform the outcome before reporting anything
  • Fine in a large sample; check outliers and note the skew
  • Fine, because a significant test shows normality holds

One participant has a Cook’s distance far above all others, and the salt slope changes from 1.8 to 1.2 when this person is removed. What should you do first?

  • Check the record for errors, then report fits with and without
  • Ignore it because one of 600 participants is too few to matter
  • Add a quadratic term until the point is no longer flagged
  • Delete the person from the dataset and refit quietly

Put a sensible assumption-checking routine in order, from first to last:

  1. Plot residuals against fitted values and each predictor
  2. Report the checks and any changes made
  3. Compare results before and after the fix
  4. Match each problem to its remedy and refit
  5. Check the Q–Q plot, VIFs and Cook’s distances
  6. Fit the planned model, chosen before looking at diagnostics

Boss · Diagnose the crime scenes

Boss: flag every sentence that pairs a residual-plot finding with the wrong conclusion or fix (linear model for SBP, n = 600).

  • A curved smoother in residuals vs fitted suggests adding a spline or squared term for the predictor.
  • A funnel shape in residuals vs fitted means the residuals are not normal, so a Q–Q plot test is needed.
  • Points far above all others in the Cook’s distance plot should be checked and the model refitted without them.
  • Residuals clustered by village mean the independence assumption holds because villages are different.
  • Two predictors with a VIF of 10 should both stay in so that each effect can be reported.
  • A slightly heavy upper tail on the Q–Q plot in a sample of 600 is not by itself a reason to abandon the model.
