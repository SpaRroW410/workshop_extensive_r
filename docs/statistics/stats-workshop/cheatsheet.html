Statistical Tests Cheat Sheet

Choose · check · test · interpret · NCD screening practice data · print on 2 pages (A4)

1 · Four questions choose the test

  1. Outcome: category or number? 2. Groups: one, two, or more? 3. Paired (same people again) or independent? 4. Assumptions met?

2 · Category outcome

Situation Test R Report
One proportion vs benchmark binomial / goodness of fit binom.test(x, n); chisq.test(tab, p = ...) % (95% CI)
2 × 2, all expected ≥ 5 chi-square chisq.test(tab, correct = FALSE) risk difference or OR (95% CI)
2 × 2, any expected < 5 Fisher’s exact fisher.test(tab) OR (95% CI)
r × c, ≥ 80% expected ≥ 5, none < 1 chi-square chisq.test(tab); $stdres Cramér’s V; cells with |residual| > 2
r × c, small expected Monte Carlo chisq.test(tab, simulate.p.value = TRUE, B = 10000) Cramér’s V
Paired yes/no McNemar mcnemar.test(tab); exact2x2::mcnemar.exact(tab) % before and after

Expected counts: chisq.test(tab)$expected. The rule is about expected, not observed, counts.

3 · Number outcome

Design Roughly normal Skewed / outliers / ordinal
One group vs a value t.test(x, mu = 100) wilcox.test(x, mu = 100)
Two independent groups Welch t.test(y ~ g) Mann–Whitney wilcox.test(y ~ g, conf.int = TRUE)
Two paired measurements t.test(a, b, paired = TRUE) Wilcoxon signed-rank wilcox.test(a, b, paired = TRUE)
≥ 3 independent groups aov(); Welch: oneway.test() kruskal.test(y ~ g)
≥ 3 repeated measurements rstatix::anova_test(dv, wid, within) friedman.test(y, time, id)
Two numbers cor.test(x, y) (Pearson) cor.test(x, y, method = "spearman")

Post hoc: Tukey TukeyHSD(); Games–Howell rstatix::games_howell_test(); Dunn rstatix::dunn_test(..., p.adjust.method = "holm"); repeated measures: pairwise.t.test(..., paired = TRUE).

4 · Checking assumptions

Assumption Check If not met
Independence the design paired / repeated-measures test; clustered data → workshop A4
Normal: within groups, the differences (paired), the residuals (ANOVA) Q-Q plot stat_qq(); skewness; Shapiro–Wilk; Lilliefors nortest::lillie.test() log scale, or a rank-based test
Equal spread compare SDs; car::leveneTest() Welch (default)
Sphericity (repeated) Mauchly in anova_test() Greenhouse–Geisser p[GG]
Expected counts chisq.test()$expected Fisher, Monte Carlo
  • ks.test(x, "pnorm") compares with mean 0, SD 1: always “not normal”. Use lillie.test(x)
  • Large n: normality tests flag trivial departures. Small n: they miss big ones. Never test a random subsample
  • z-test only with a known population SD; otherwise t, whatever n
  • pairwise.t.test() after repeated measures without paired = TRUE

Test Effect size R Rough guide

Mann–Whitney, Wilcoxon Hodges–Lehmann shift; r wilcox.test(conf.int = TRUE); rstatix::wilcox_effsize() r 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5

Kruskal–Wallis, Friedman ε² / η²[H]; Kendall’s W rstatix::kruskal_effsize(), friedman_effsize() W 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5
Chi-square risk difference, OR; Cramér’s V prop.test()$conf.int; effectsize::cramers_v() V 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5
Correlation r (95% CI); r² cor.test() r 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5

6 · Reading a result

You see You say
p < 0.05, CI excludes 0 (or 1) evidence of a difference; give its size and CI
p ≥ 0.05, narrow CI no evidence of a difference; any difference is small
p ≥ 0.05, wide CI cannot tell: too little information
p = 0.056 weak evidence (not “a trend”, not “nearly significant”)
tiny p, small effect real but small; judge clinical importance
r = 0.35 moderate: r² = 12% of variation; not cause, not agreement

Many tests: plan the main ones; p.adjust(p, method = "holm"); label the rest exploratory.

7 · One sentence per result

Adults with a past history of diabetes were older: mean 53.8 vs 42.9 years (difference 10.9 years, 95% CI 6.4 to 15.4; Welch t-test, p < 0.001).
