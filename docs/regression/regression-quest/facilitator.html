Facilitator pack

Run Dungeons & Dataframes with a cohort: formats, session plans, discussion prompts, pilot protocol

For faculty and senior residents who run the game with a group. Everything stays in each learner’s browser; there is no server and no dashboard yet (that is Phase 6). To see a cohort at a glance, collect each learner’s exported progress file and open the cohort summary tool.

1 · Two ways to run it

Format Shape Best for
A · Guided lab session One 2-hour session in a computer room, after the one-day regression workshop Residents who just attended the workshop
B · Self-paced weeks Worlds 1–6 over about 6 weeks, one world per week, 30 to 45 minutes each, plus a 20-minute check-in Part-time learners, journal-club style groups

Learners need a browser only. R is assumed (basic-to-moderate); anyone unsure takes the readiness check first. It advises and never blocks.

2 · Before you start (10 minutes)

  1. Open the Campus and each world page on a learner machine to confirm they load offline or on the department network.
  2. Agree pseudonymous file names (for example dr-01, dr-02). Do not use names or hospital numbers.
  3. Decide whether you want calm mode for the group (hides streaks and pop-ups; learners can switch it on from the Campus).
  4. Tell learners that scores are for learning, that hints cost XP but never marks, and that “show answer” is always allowed.

3 · Format A: a 2-hour session plan

Time Activity Notes
0:00 Frame the game (5 min) XP rewards understanding, not speed. Bosses are the mastery check.
0:05 Pre-test (20 min, silent) 20 items, confidence rating on each. No feedback while answering.
0:25 World 3 Confounding Caves (25 min) Pairs. Pause after Level 2 for the discussion prompt below.
0:50 Break (10 min)
1:00 World 4 Assumption Ruins (25 min) Show check_model() on the NCD data next to the plot items.
1:25 Boss round (15 min) Each pair defends one flagged sentence aloud.
1:40 Debrief (10 min) Cohort weakest skills (use the cohort tool); agree what to do next week.
1:50 Pointers Worlds 5 and 6, post-test in 2 to 4 weeks.

Worlds 1 and 2 are best done as homework before the session.

4. World by world: timing, prompt, misconception

World Time Discussion prompt Misconception to listen for
1 · Outcome Detective 30 min “Which model family would you pick for each outcome in your thesis?” The predictors choose the model, not the outcome
2 · Straight Line 35 min “Why is a prediction interval so much wider than a confidence interval?” A slope is a promise about one person
3 · Confounding Caves 40 min “A slope falls from 3.0 to 1.8 after adjustment. What does that tell you, and what does it not?” Adjusting removes all confounding; dropping variables that “shrink” the slope
4 · Assumption Ruins 40 min “Which residual plot would make you change your analysis, and which would not?” Normality tests decide everything; deleting influential people
5 · Odds Arena 40 min “Convert an OR of 2.0 into a prevalence ratio for a 30% outcome. Which would you report?” An OR is ‘times as likely’; pseudo-R² is variance explained
6 · Reporting Guild 40 min “Rewrite the worst sentence in the boss paragraph the way you would for a reviewer.” Significant means important; causal verbs in cross-sectional data

Every world ends with a boss (a spot-the-flaw item). If a group clears it in one go, ask them to write a new flawed sentence for the next group.

5. Pilot protocol (Phase 2 exit)

The roadmap’s Phase 2 exit is a pilot of 20 or more residents showing a measurable pre to post gain.

  1. Recruit 20+ residents; record only a pseudonym, year of training and prior R experience.
  2. Day 0: readiness check, then pre-test. Learners export their progress file (Campus or index page, Export).
  3. Weeks 1 to 6: Worlds 3 to 6 (and 1 to 2 if new). Keep a short log of questions asked and where people got stuck.
  4. Post-test 2 to 4 weeks after the last world. Export again.
  5. Think-aloud: watch 3 to 5 learners work through one world and note item wording that confuses people and any item that takes more than 3 minutes.
  6. Analyse with the cohort tool: mean gain, learners with no gain, weakest skills. Report items most often missed first-try to the item author for review.
  7. Decide: if mean gain is positive and no item is systematically misread, Phase 2 passes. If not, revise those items and re-pilot.

What to record per item (for the author)

  • Stem or option the learners misread (quote it)
  • Whether the explanation after a wrong answer was enough
  • Time taken, and whether hints were used

6. Cheat sheet and printouts

The two-page interpretation cheat sheet from the workshop is the take-home companion to Worlds 2 to 6. Print it for the session.

7. Privacy and good practice

  • Progress is stored in the learner’s own browser and moves only when they export it. The cohort tool reads files locally and uploads nothing.
  • Use pseudonymous file names, store exports on a department drive, and delete them after the analysis.
  • This is a learning tool: do not use scores for grading or promotion. Certificates come later (Phase 6, with server-side checking).
