Data Viz Caveats: Flash Cards

Author

Sparrow

Published

November 22, 2026

A checklist of the mistakes that most often make a chart mislead or confuse, as 70 flash cards in 8 groups. Read the title, decide what the problem is and how you would fix it, then open the card. Use Practise to be dealt the cards one at a time; the ones you mark Review again come back first.

Most of these points are taught in Best Practices, Matching Data to Plots and the ggplot2 modules; each card links to where. The list of topics follows the caveats collection of data-to-viz.com by Yan Holtz and Conor Healy, where many cards have a longer illustrated page (the “data-to-viz” link on the card). The wording and the R code here are our own.

Misleading charts

Do not cut the y-axis of a bar chart

Problem. Bars encode values by their length. Starting the axis at 120 mmHg makes a 3 mmHg gap look like a doubling.

Fix. Start bar charts at zero. If the differences matter but are small, show points with confidence intervals instead of bars.

ggplot(d, aes(group, sbp)) + geom_col() + scale_y_continuous(limits = c(0, NA))

In the course: Best Practices · data-to-viz

Box plots hide the shape

Problem. Two groups can have identical boxes while one is bimodal and the other is not; small groups look as solid as large ones.

Fix. Add the raw points (jittered), or use a violin or raincloud, and print the n per group.

geom_boxplot(outlier.shape = NA) + geom_jitter(width = 0.15, alpha = 0.4)

In the course: Merged Visuals · data-to-viz

Avoid counter-intuitive encodings

Problem. Readers expect up = more, red = bad, bigger = larger. An inverted axis or a reversed colour scale makes them read the opposite of the truth.

Fix. Keep the usual direction; if you must invert (e.g. ranks), say so on the axis and in the title.

data-to-viz

Bars with error bars (“dynamite plots”) hide the data

Problem. A bar of the mean with an error bar shows two numbers and suggests values below the mean are the only ones that exist.

Fix. Show the individual points with the mean and its 95% CI, or a box/violin plot. State whether bars are SD, SE or CI.

stat_summary(fun.data = mean_cl_normal, geom = 'pointrange') + geom_jitter(alpha = 0.3)

In the course: Statistical Plots · data-to-viz

Be wary of two y-axes

Problem. The scales of the two axes are arbitrary, so any two series can be made to cross, diverge or move together.

Fix. Use two panels with a shared x-axis, or index both series to 100 at the start.

facet_wrap(~ series, ncol = 1, scales = 'free_y')

In the course: Matching Data to Plots · data-to-viz

Avoid 3D

Problem. Perspective makes nearer bars and slices look bigger and hides some values behind others.

Fix. Use the 2D version. Three numeric variables? Colour or size for the third, or small multiples.

In the course: Matching Data to Plots · data-to-viz

Do not cherry-pick the data

Problem. Choosing the start date, the sites or the subgroup that tells the best story misleads even when every point is correct.

Fix. Show the full period and all groups, and say why any are left out.

Draw proportions accurately

Problem. Icons or shapes scaled by height and width (both) exaggerate differences; a doubled value looks four times larger.

Fix. Make area (not radius or height and width together) proportional to the value, or simply use bars.

Respect time intervals

Problem. Unevenly spaced dates (2010, 2015, 2020, 2021, 2022) drawn at equal distances distort the trend.

Fix. Use a real date or numeric x-axis so the gaps are drawn to scale.

aes(x = as.Date(visit_date))   # not factor(visit_date)
Show the sample size

Problem. A percentage from 8 people looks as solid as one from 800.

Fix. Print the n on the axis labels, in the legend or in a caption; add confidence intervals.

scale_x_discrete(labels = \(x) paste0(x, '\n(n = ', table(d$group)[x], ')'))
Beware of Simpson’s paradox

Problem. A trend in the pooled data can reverse inside every subgroup when the groups differ in size and level.

Fix. Colour or facet by the likely confounder before drawing a conclusion; adjust for it in the model.

ggplot(d, aes(x, y, colour = group)) + geom_point() + geom_smooth(method = 'lm')

Wrong chart type

Many thin lollipops or bars can shimmer

Problem. Dozens of closely spaced thin lines create a moiré pattern that is tiring to look at.

Fix. Use fewer, thicker bars, or a dot plot, or aggregate into groups.

The trouble with pie charts

Problem. People compare angles and areas poorly; slices of 23% and 27% look the same, and more than 5 slices are unreadable.

Fix. A sorted bar chart. Keep a pie only for 2–3 parts of a whole for a general audience.

ggplot(d, aes(reorder(cause, n), n)) + geom_col() + coord_flip()

In the course: Best Practices · data-to-viz

Radial bars distort length

Problem. In a circular bar chart the outer bars are longer than inner bars of the same value.

Fix. Use straight bars; keep radial layouts for a decorative overview with labels on the values.

data-to-viz

Maybe you do not need a map

Problem. A map draws attention to large, empty areas; if geography is not the question, it wastes space.

Fix. A sorted bar or dot chart of the regions answers “which is highest?” better than a map.

In the course: Maps and Choropleths

Two values per group? Try a dumbbell

Problem. Grouped bars for before/after (or men/women) make the reader compare bars across pairs.

Fix. A dumbbell (two points joined by a line) shows both values and the change in one glance.

geom_segment(aes(x = before, xend = after, y = site, yend = site)) + geom_point(aes(before, site)) + geom_point(aes(after, site))

In the course: Showcase

Use circular bar plots with care

Problem. Without a radial axis readers cannot read values, and the shape is driven by the order of the bars.

Fix. Label each bar with its value and sort it; prefer a straight bar chart for comparison.

Colour

Use colour on purpose

Problem. Colouring every bar differently adds a legend and implies groups that mean nothing.

Fix. One colour by default; add colour only to encode a variable or to highlight the message (grey + one accent).

geom_col(aes(fill = site == 'Hospital B')) + scale_fill_manual(values = c('grey70', '#C0392B'), guide = 'none')

In the course: Best Practices · data-to-viz

Choose the background on purpose

Problem. A dark or coloured panel background reduces contrast and changes how colours look.

Fix. White or very light grey backgrounds; theme_minimal() or theme_classic().

Keep enough contrast

Problem. Light-yellow lines or pale text on white disappear on a projector and in print.

Fix. Check contrast (text should be dark); avoid the lightest end of a palette for thin lines and text.

Avoid pure, saturated colours

Problem. Pure red, green and blue (#FF0000 …) vibrate next to each other and tire the eye.

Fix. Muted palettes (viridis, ColorBrewer, Okabe-Ito) with one stronger colour for emphasis.

Numbers need a sequential palette

Problem. A rainbow or qualitative palette on a numeric variable has no visual order.

Fix. One hue from light to dark (or viridis) so darker = more.

scale_fill_viridis_c()   # or scale_fill_distiller(palette = 'Blues', direction = 1)

In the course: ggplot2 Module 3

Too many colours kill the story

Problem. Beyond about 6 colours readers cannot match lines to the legend.

Fix. Highlight 1–3 groups and grey the rest, or use small multiples.

In the course: Best Practices

No gradient for unordered groups

Problem. A light-to-dark gradient on regions or treatments suggests an order that does not exist.

Fix. A qualitative palette (scale_colour_brewer(palette = ‘Set2’) or Okabe-Ito) for categories.

Avoid the rainbow palette

Problem. Rainbow scales have sudden bright bands (yellow, cyan) that create boundaries not in the data, and fail in greyscale.

Fix. viridis or a single-hue sequential scale.

data-to-viz

Diverging data, diverging palette

Problem. Change from baseline, residuals and correlations have a meaningful zero that a sequential palette hides.

Fix. Two hues meeting at a neutral colour placed exactly at the midpoint.

scale_fill_gradient2(low = '#2166AC', mid = 'white', high = '#B2182B', midpoint = 0)

In the course: ggplot2 Module 3

Think of colour-blind readers

Problem. About 1 in 12 men cannot tell red from green.

Fix. Colour-blind-safe palettes (viridis, Okabe-Ito), plus redundant cues (shape, direct labels).

scale_colour_viridis_d()

Design

Align everything with something

Problem. Titles, panels and annotations that float at random positions look messy and hard to scan.

Fix. Left-align title, subtitle and caption with the plot; use the same margins across panels.

theme(plot.title.position = 'plot', plot.caption.position = 'plot')
Two fonts are enough

Problem. Many fonts and styles compete for attention.

Fix. One font family; vary size and weight for hierarchy.

Do you need that box?

Problem. Borders around the panel, legend and plot add ink without information.

Fix. Remove panel borders and legend boxes; let white space separate elements.

theme(panel.border = element_blank(), legend.background = element_blank())
Font size matters

Problem. Default 11 pt axis text is unreadable on a slide or a printed half-page.

Fix. Set base_size for the output (about 14–18 for slides); check the figure at its final size.

theme_minimal(base_size = 16)
Group what belongs together

Problem. Related bars or panels far apart force the eye to jump.

Fix. Order categories so comparisons are adjacent; group panels by the question they answer.

Make the title say something

Problem. A title like “SBP by group” makes the reader find the message themselves.

Fix. State the finding in the title (“SBP fell by 9 mmHg with the intervention”) and keep the description in the subtitle.

In the course: Best Practices

Declutter

Problem. Heavy grids, tick marks, borders and legends compete with the data.

Fix. Light or no grid lines, direct labels instead of legends, no redundant text.

In the course: Best Practices · data-to-viz

Maps

Do not move regions around

Problem. Moving or resizing islands and remote areas to fit the frame changes their apparent size and position.

Fix. If you must inset a region, frame it and say so; keep the same projection.

Mind the projection

Problem. Web Mercator inflates areas far from the equator, so a choropleth over-weights them.

Fix. Use an equal-area or local projected CRS for thematic maps (e.g. UTM for a district).

st_transform(blocks, 32644)

In the course: Spatial Data with sf

Normalise your maps

Problem. A map of counts is mostly a map of where people live.

Fix. Map rates (per 100,000) or ratios to expected; show counts only when workload is the question.

mutate(rate = cases / population * 1e5)

In the course: Maps and Choropleths · data-to-viz

Show missing data as missing

Problem. A missing area coloured like the lowest class looks like “no disease”.

Fix. Give missing areas a distinct neutral colour or hatching and a legend entry.

scale_fill_viridis_c(na.value = 'grey85')
Do not let small regions vanish

Problem. Dense urban wards are tiny on a map, though they hold most people.

Fix. Add an inset or zoom, a cartogram, or a hexagon tile map.

Labels and legends

Prefer horizontal labels

Problem. Rotated axis labels are hard to read.

Fix. Flip the axes so long category names sit horizontally.

ggplot(d, aes(n, reorder(diagnosis, n))) + geom_col()

In the course: Best Practices · data-to-viz

Use legends sparingly

Problem. A legend makes the eye jump back and forth.

Fix. Label lines and groups directly at their ends; drop the legend.

geom_text(data = last_points, aes(label = group), hjust = 0) + theme(legend.position = 'none')
Do not put labels on top of the data

Problem. Text over points or lines hides what it describes.

Fix. Offset labels, use ggrepel, or place them in empty space.

ggrepel::geom_text_repel(aes(label = name))
Round your numbers

Problem. A value like “37.4285%” suggests a precision the data do not have and is harder to read.

Fix. Round to what matters (37%); use scales::percent() for axes.

scale_y_continuous(labels = scales::percent)
Annotation is crucial

Problem. Readers miss the event or threshold that explains the pattern.

Fix. Add a short note, a reference line or a shaded period where it matters.

geom_vline(xintercept = as.Date('2024-06-01'), linetype = 2) + annotate('text', ...)

data-to-viz

Do not label every tick

Problem. A label on every year or category crowds the axis.

Fix. Fewer, round breaks.

scale_x_date(date_breaks = '6 months', date_labels = '%b %Y')

Dashboards and data stories

Keep colours consistent across charts

Problem. If “Intervention” is blue in one panel and orange in the next, readers mis-match them.

Fix. A named colour vector used by every plot.

arm_cols <- c(Control = 'grey50', Intervention = '#2C5F8D'); scale_colour_manual(values = arm_cols)

data-to-viz

Share the y-axis to compare panels

Problem. Panels with their own y range look similar even when one is ten times higher.

Fix. Keep scales fixed when the panels are compared (the facet default); free them only to show shape.

Leave margins between charts

Problem. Panels pressed together read as one figure.

Fix. Space and alignment between panels (patchwork handles both).

Link charts that share an axis

Problem. Separate charts with the same x (time) are hard to read together.

Fix. Stack them vertically with aligned x-axes, or link them interactively (crosstalk).

Other

Sort your bar chart

Problem. Alphabetical order hides the ranking.

Fix. Order bars by value (unless the categories have their own order, like age groups).

aes(x = reorder(region, rate))   # or forcats::fct_reorder()

In the course: Basic Plot Gallery · data-to-viz

Histograms - mind the bin width

Problem. The same data can look smooth, bimodal or noisy depending on the bins.

Fix. Try several widths and choose a meaningful one (5 mmHg, 1 year); report it.

geom_histogram(binwidth = 5, boundary = 0)

data-to-viz

Size bubbles by area, not radius

Problem. If the radius doubles, the area quadruples.

Fix. ggplot2’s scale_size() already maps to area; avoid scale_radius().

scale_size_area(max_size = 12)

data-to-viz

The spaghetti plot

Problem. Twenty overlapping lines are unreadable.

Fix. Grey all lines and highlight one or a few, or small multiples with the others in grey behind.

In the course: Showcase · data-to-viz

Connect only connected dots

Problem. A line implies continuity between points; joining unrelated categories invents a trend.

Fix. Lines for ordered x (time, dose); points or bars for categories.

In the course: ggplot2 Module 1 · data-to-viz

Drawing order matters

Problem. The layer drawn last hides the others: a large group can bury a small one.

Fix. Draw the highlight last; use transparency.

geom_point(data = others, colour = 'grey') + geom_point(data = highlight, colour = 'red')

In the course: ggplot2 Module 4

Overlapping sets? Use an UpSet plot

Problem. Venn diagrams with more than three sets (e.g. comorbidities) are unreadable.

Fix. An UpSet plot shows the size of every intersection as a bar.

Stacked charts are hard to read

Problem. Only the bottom segment has a common baseline; the rest cannot be compared.

Fix. Grouped bars, small multiples, or 100% stacks with few segments.

In the course: Matching Data to Plots · data-to-viz

Mind the aspect ratio

Problem. Stretching a plot changes how steep a trend looks.

Fix. Choose the width/height on purpose (about 45° for the main slopes); set it in ggsave().

ggsave('fig.png', width = 7, height = 4)

data-to-viz

Make date axes easy to read

Problem. Default date labels can be dense or ambiguous (01/02).

Fix. Unambiguous formats (“Jan 2025”) and sensible breaks.

scale_x_date(date_labels = '%b %Y')
Reorder your heat map

Problem. Rows and columns in input order hide any structure.

Fix. Order by a summary or by clustering so similar rows sit together.

aes(y = fct_reorder(ward, cases, .fun = sum))

In the course: Showcase · data-to-viz

Beware of overplotting

Problem. With thousands of points the scatter becomes a solid blob.

Fix. Transparency, smaller points, hexbins or 2D density.

geom_point(alpha = 0.2)   # or geom_hex()

In the course: Intermediate Plot Gallery · data-to-viz

Too many distributions at once

Problem. Overlaid densities or histograms for many groups become a tangle.

Fix. A ridgeline plot, small multiples or box plots side by side.

ggridges::geom_density_ridges()

data-to-viz

Small multiples - rows or columns?

Problem. The layout decides what is easy to compare: aligned y values compare across a row, aligned x values down a column.

Fix. Put panels in one row to compare heights, in one column to compare positions on x.

facet_wrap(~ group, nrow = 1)

data-to-viz

Check the numbers on the chart

Problem. Percentages that do not add to 100, or totals that do not match the table, destroy trust.

Fix. Compute labels from the data in code; never type them by hand.

data-to-viz

Do not make readers do arithmetic

Problem. If the message is a difference, two lines make the reader subtract in their head.

Fix. Plot the difference itself (with its CI).

data-to-viz

Area is a weak encoding

Problem. People underestimate area differences; bubbles and treemaps give rough impressions only.

Fix. Use position or length when exact comparison matters.

data-to-viz

Grouped bars must look grouped

Problem. Evenly spaced bars hide which belong together.

Fix. Dodge within a group and leave a gap between groups.

geom_col(position = position_dodge(width = 0.8), width = 0.7)

data-to-viz

Radar (spider) charts mislead

Problem. The area depends on the order of the axes, and values are hard to read.

Fix. A dot plot or parallel coordinates for several measures per item.

data-to-viz

Log scales for rates of change

Problem. On a linear axis exponential growth looks explosive and early changes look flat.

Fix. A log axis when relative change is the question; label it clearly.

scale_y_log10(labels = scales::comma)
It is “choropleth”

Problem. The common misspelling “chloropleth” undermines a report.

Fix. choro (area) + pleth (quantity).

Self-check quiz

A chart shows the mean SBP of two arms as bars with error bars, the y-axis starting at 120 mmHg. How many caveats does it break?

  • None: the error bars make it an honest chart
  • One: the error bars alone are wrong
  • Two: the cut axis and the bar of means
  • One: just the axis start is wrong

You map dengue cases (counts) per block. What should you map instead, and why?

  • Cases per 100,000, so population does not drive it
  • The log of the counts, to compress the colour scale
  • Cases per km², so that large blocks look smaller
  • The rank of each count, so classes have equal size

Twenty ward lines overlap on one time-series chart. Which fix keeps every ward visible?

  • Give each of the wards its own bright, saturated colour
  • Small multiples, each ward over the rest in grey
  • A 3D chart with one ward per depth level
  • A dual y-axis for the larger and smaller wards

Change in SBP from baseline (negative and positive) is shown on a map. Which palette fits?

  • A rainbow palette from red to violet
  • A single-hue palette from light to dark blue
  • A qualitative palette with one hue per block
  • A diverging palette with white at zero
